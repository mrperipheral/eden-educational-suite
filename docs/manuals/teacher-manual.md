# Teacher Manual

A practical guide for the **Teacher** role. As a Teacher, you only see and
act on the specific classes and subjects you've been **assigned** to by
your School Admin/Principal (Teachers → your profile → assignments) — this
manual only covers what you can actually do.

---

## Login

1. Go to the sign-in page, enter your **Email address** and **Password**,
   click **Sign in**.
2. Forgot your password? Use **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page.
3. If you can't get in, check with your School Admin — your account may
   not yet have the Teacher role, or may be suspended.

## Dashboard

After signing in you land on the **Dashboard** — a snapshot of the whole
school (active students/staff counts, recent admin activity if you have
audit access, and KPI cards if the Reports module is on and you hold the
relevant permission). Your own working area is reached through the left
navigation, not the dashboard itself.

## Your assigned subjects/classes

You don't manage this yourself — your School Admin/Principal sets your
**assignments** (which class + subject you teach) from your profile under
**Teachers**. Everywhere you record attendance, scores, CBT content, or
learning materials, you'll only ever see the classes/subjects you've been
assigned to. If a class you expect to teach doesn't appear, ask your
School Admin to check your assignment.

## Timetable

**Purpose:** See your own weekly teaching schedule.
**Prerequisites:** Timetable module on (your school may not have this
switched on — it's an optional module).

**Steps:** Go to **Timetable** in the left navigation → your own
teacher-view schedule shows your periods across the week.

## Attendance

**Purpose:** Take daily attendance for your assigned class(es).
**Prerequisites:** Attendance module on; you must be assigned to the
class.

**Steps:**
1. Go to **Attendance** → **Create register** (only your assigned classes
   appear in the level/arm choices).
2. Choose session, term, level, arm, and the date (cannot be a future
   date).
3. Click **Create register** — today's roster is captured automatically.
4. Mark each student **Present / Absent / Late / Excused** — click the
   single-letter buttons, or **Mark all present** then adjust exceptions.
   Add a note if useful.
5. Click **Save draft** to keep working later, or **Save & submit** once
   everyone is marked (this button stays disabled until every student has
   a mark).

**Expected result:** A submitted register is locked — if you need to
correct it afterward, ask your School Admin/Principal to **Reopen for
correction** (only they can do that).

**Troubleshooting:** If the class you want isn't in the dropdown, you're
not assigned to it — check with your School Admin.

## Assessments

**Purpose:** Create assessments and enter scores for your assigned
class/subject.
**Prerequisites:** Assessments module on; at least one assessment category
exists (your School Admin sets these up).

**Steps — create one:**
1. Go to **Assessments** → **New assessment**.
2. Choose the class/subject (only your assignments appear), category
   (e.g. Classwork, Test, Exam), maximum score, a title, and the
   assessment date.
3. Click **Create assessment** — the roster on that date is captured.

**Steps — enter scores:**
1. Open the assessment → **Score sheet**.
2. Type each student's score (leave blank for not-yet-scored), add an
   optional comment.
3. Click **Save scores** — you can come back and edit while the assessment
   is still a **draft**.
4. Once scoring is complete, **Publish** it, then **Lock** it — a locked
   assessment's scores are what feed into the class's Results.

**Common mistakes:** A score above the assessment's own maximum is
rejected — check the "out of :n" hint next to the score sheet. Once
locked, you can no longer change scores yourself — ask your School
Admin/Principal to unlock it if a genuine correction is needed.

## Assignments

**Purpose:** Set homework with a due date; track who's submitted (no file
upload — completion status only).

**Steps:**
1. Go to **Assessments** → **Assignments** → **Create**.
2. Set class, subject, due date, instructions.
3. **Publish** — now visible to students/parents.
4. Open **Submissions** to mark each student pending/submitted/late/
   exempt as work comes in.
5. **Close** it once done.

## Assignment submissions

See the "Assignments" steps above — you mark submission status yourself
from the assignment's **Submissions** screen; students don't upload files
in this version.

## Learning materials

**Purpose:** Upload a file (PDF/image/audio) for your assigned
class/subject.
**Prerequisites:** Learning Materials module on (optional module — may not
be enabled at your school).

**Steps:**
1. Go to **Learning Materials** → **Upload**.
2. Choose **Session**, optional **Term**, and pick **Class & subject**
   from **Select one of your assigned classes** (a dropdown of your own
   assignments, not a free class/subject picker).
3. Enter a **Title**, optional description, and choose the **File** (PDF,
   JPG/PNG/WEBP, or MP3/M4A/WAV — max 20MB; video isn't supported).
4. Click **Upload & save**.

**Expected result:** Students in that class can view/download it (video
files are rejected even if renamed — the system checks the real file
content).

## Question Bank

**Purpose:** Write reusable CBT questions for your subject.
**Prerequisites:** CBT module on.

**Steps:** Go to **CBT** → **Questions** → **Add question**. Write the
question, choose multiple-choice or true/false, enter options and mark
the correct one(s), optionally set a subject/level/arm scope, topic, and
difficulty. Only **Active** questions can be attached to a new exam — use
**Deactivate**/**Archive** to retire one (this never affects an exam that
already used it).

## CBT (creating/managing exams for your class)

**Purpose:** Build and schedule an online exam for a class/subject you
teach.
**Prerequisites:** CBT module on; you must be assigned to the class +
subject.

**Steps:**
1. Go to **CBT** → **Create examination**.
2. Choose your class/subject (only your assignments appear), duration,
   time window, pass mark, and result release (immediate or scheduled).
3. Save as **Draft**, add questions (write new ones or pull from the
   Question Bank).
4. Click **Schedule** — locks the question set, makes it visible to
   students within its time window. You can no longer edit questions once
   scheduled.
5. **Close** it when done, or let it close automatically at the end of its
   window. View **Attempts & results** for the full list — you always see
   every student's actual score, regardless of the result-release setting
   (that setting only controls what the **student** sees, and when).

**Common mistakes:** Trying to add/remove a question after scheduling —
not possible; editing questions requires the exam still be a **Draft**.

## Results visibility

**Purpose:** See compiled results for your assigned class, and add a
class-teacher comment.
**Prerequisites:** Results module on; the result run must be compiled.

**Steps:** Go to **Results** — you see runs for your own assigned classes.
Open one to see each student's compiled percentage, grade, and position
(if ranking is on). You can add/edit a **class-teacher comment** on a
student's result while you're assigned to that class — you cannot compile,
approve, publish, or lock a run yourself; that's School Admin/Principal
work.

## Communication

**Purpose:** Log parent communication and respond to threads.
**Prerequisites:** Notifications module on.

**Steps:** Go to **Communication** → **New thread** to log a call/visit/
concern (optionally link the student/guardian). Use **Resolve** or
**Escalate** on an existing thread as it progresses. You also see
published **Announcements** relevant to staff.

## Profile / security

Click your name (top-right) → **Account settings** to update your name,
email, or password. Use **Switch school** if you teach at more than one
school on this platform. **Sign out** from the same menu.
